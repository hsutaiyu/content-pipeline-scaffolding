# The Conversation: "An AI agent spent your money. Can anyone prove you authorized it?"

**What happened:** Uses a scenario where a user tells an agent to find a shirt under $30 "but do not buy it", and it buys anyway. Argues no single company's records can show what the user authorised, and lists what accountability would need, including verifiable linkage across all parties and tamper-evident records. Cites AP2 as showing "how the evidence could travel", and the Warner bill (item 14) as falling short of "a verifiable evidence chain across different systems involved."

**Thesis tie:** The most direct statement of the thesis problem in this area: the evidence of one event is split across independent systems.

**Post angle:** An academic asks a plain question: if an agent buys something you said not to, whose records prove it? Good framing piece to link from a carousel.

**Caution:** Commentary, not primary. Scenario is hypothetical.

**Source:** The Conversation, Aashis Luitel (University of the Cumberlands), 13 August 2026
