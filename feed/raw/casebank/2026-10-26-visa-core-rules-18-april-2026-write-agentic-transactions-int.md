# Visa Core Rules (18 April 2026) write agentic transactions into the rulebook

**What happened:** Visa's public rulebook now defines an "Agentic Transaction" as an e-commerce transaction undertaken by an "Agentic Payment Provider" on behalf of a cardholder, based on the cardholder-defined payment instruction. Before transacting, the provider must obtain cardholder consent and "clearly state the expiration date of the Cardholder-defined payment instruction". After the transaction, the provider must make an order confirmation available to the cardholder "for at least 120 days from the Processing Date", including the goods, merchant contact details, total price and currency.

**Thesis tie:** Record bundled with governance. The record of what the agent did is kept by the agent provider under Visa's rules, for a fixed window, and Visa's rules decide what it means. Strong primary source for "each party keeps its own log".

**Post angle:** Visa now tells agent providers to keep an order confirmation for 120 days and treats the agent's actions as the cardholder's own. A careful, factual example of a network doing the coordination work: it decides who keeps which record and for how long.

**Caution:** Long legal document; quote section numbers precisely. Do not read the cardholder-responsibility clause as Visa shifting blame onto consumers; it sits alongside existing dispute rights.

**Source:** "Visa Core Rules and Visa Product and Service Rules", Visa, edition dated 18 April 2026 (public PDF)
