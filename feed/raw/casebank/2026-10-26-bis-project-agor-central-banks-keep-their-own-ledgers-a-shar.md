# BIS Project Agorá: central banks keep their own ledgers, a shared one coordinates

**What happened:** Agorá is led by the BIS with seven central banks (Bank of England, New York Fed, Banque de France for the Eurosystem, Bank of Japan, Bank of Korea, Bank of Mexico, Swiss National Bank), the Bank of Canada joining in 2026, and more than 40 private firms. Its prototype put tokenised commercial bank deposits on a shared "unifying ledger" and tokenised central bank reserves on separate jurisdictional ledgers, each under its own central bank. The BIS says atomic cross-border settlement worked, and the project moves next to real-value testing.

**Thesis tie:** Sovereign domains deriving their own state. This is the clearest institutional statement in this set: shared coordination, with the authoritative state kept in each jurisdiction under its own rules.

**Post angle:** Seven central banks designed a shared payments platform, and their first design decision was that none of them would hand its own ledger to the shared one.

**Caution:** Search results report real-value test figures (about CHF 800,000, about 80 seconds) that were not on the fetched BIS page. Leave them out unless verified against the report. Agorá is not a production system.
