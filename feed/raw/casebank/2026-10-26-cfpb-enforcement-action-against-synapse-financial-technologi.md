# CFPB enforcement action against Synapse Financial Technologies

**What happened:** Synapse was middleware that sat between consumer fintech apps and partner banks holding pooled "for benefit of" accounts. It filed for Chapter 11 on 22 April 2024. The CFPB alleged that Synapse failed to keep adequate records of where consumers' funds were and failed to make those records match its partner banks' records. According to the CFPB, the banks found they held $60 million to $90 million less than Synapse's records said they should. The settlement set a $1 civil penalty, which let the CFPB pay victims from its Civil Penalty Fund (reported elsewhere as $46 million).

**Thesis tie:** Reconciliation across boundaries; events vs state. The fintech ledger and each bank's ledger were separate derived states with no shared record of the underlying events, so when they diverged nobody had an authoritative sequence to rebuild from.

**Post angle:** A regulator writing, in a federal complaint, that the failure was two sets of records not matching. Actor: the partner banks. Decision: reconcile against Synapse's ledger before releasing funds. Consequence: consumers waited weeks to months for the reconciliation to finish.

**Caution:** Litigation and consumer harm. Do not name Synapse as a villain or imply a different architecture would have prevented the shortfall. The $46 million payout figure comes from secondary press (PYMNTS), not from this page.

**Source:** Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, enforcement action page "Synapse Financial Technologies, Inc." Complaint filed 21 August 2025; stipulated final judgment entered 12 September 2025
