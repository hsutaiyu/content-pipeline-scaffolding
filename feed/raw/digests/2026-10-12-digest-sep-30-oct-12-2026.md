# Digest — Sep 30–Oct 12, 2026

**TL;DR:** 6 calls. Formal-verification researchers see OV as a natural home for agent-assurance work; hardest push came on the tokenless base layer's economics and staying user-oriented.

Period: Sep 30 – Oct 12, 2026 · Calls covered: 6
Biggest recurring theme: formal-verification researchers (Nikhil Swamy, Bas Spitters, Ning Luo) see OV as a natural home for agent-assurance work, while mechanism-design and cryptography researchers (Ke Wu, Philipp Jovanovic) pushed hardest on the tokenless base layer's economics and on connecting research to concrete use cases.
## Tokenless & Permissionless Base Layer
- Ke Wu (University of Michigan, blockchain mechanism design) — strongest technical engagement; his own research is exactly "designing mechanisms for rewarding base layer participants without" tokens: "I was really excited when I see the call." Laid out three possible meanings of "tokenless": (1) no slashing/staking, (2) no native currency but fiat-paid monitoring incentives, (3) no transfers at all, with utility only from service-to-receive-service design — and asked which layer OV means.
- Ke Wu questioned whether an open, permissionless set of ordering/storage nodes is compatible with a reputation-based system: "I wouldn't call them open permissionless participation for who can be ordering nodes"; asked who ultimately stores the ordering so others can retrieve it.
- Bas Spitters (Aarhus University) asked directly whether OV is "a layer 2 on Ethereum" or its own blockchain; confirmed the service/protocol framing: "So it's a protocol that people would run themselves."
## Consensus & Ordering
- Ke Wu: "How much of it do you think is freeze?" — how much of the consensus structure is fixed.
- Ke Wu challenged total ordering: "Do you really want a total ordering of all the events... because we don't really need a total ordering of all the events." Proposed Merkle proofs as the retrieval mechanism for local ordering: "I have a local ordering. I have a Merkle proof."
- Bas Spitters said the consensus, identity, and election protocols OV needs are "things we've done before... but I can see how to think about that," including Lean-based probabilistic modeling for an EasyCrypt-like system.
## Formal Verification & Agent Assurance
- Nikhil Swamy (Microsoft Research, F* creator) — most striking voice: "the scary part is that these agents can break software at an alarming rate. But the good news is that the same agents that do the breaking can also do the proving." Reported "in the last several months, I've proved more software than I've proven in my whole life by a factor of 10."
- Nikhil Swamy warned against "specification slop": agents may do whatever slop they want to carry out a proof, "but at the end of the day, the spec has to be something that you can understand and explain to someone else." Core framing: constrain the human-agent-FV interface so an auditor "can make sense of what was actually proven."
- Ning Luo (UIUC ECE) presented her group's "proof carrying agent" proposal — verifies authorization, requires the operator to provide proofs about the operator's and agent's actions; offered to share slides.
- Ning Luo proposed specifying each agent's access, then modeling the agent network to reason about collusion under particular access controls; suggested agents should have no read/write rights until explicitly granted (OS-style user/group/root permission model); reacted positively to a fine-grained, standardized access-control layer for agents independent of host software.
- Bas Spitters asked which building blocks OV wants verified and whether OV has anything like Ethereum's FV/ZK program; background is Rocq, now Lean on probabilistic systems and quantum modeling, aiming at something analogous to "PQ EasyCrypt."
## Post-Quantum Cryptography
- Bas Spitters' main current work: post-quantum cryptography with the Ethereum Foundation and AI foundations, plus formal verification for the quantum software stack; raised PQ primitives as a verification target.
## Agent Identity, Trust & Adoption
- Bas Spitters: "What's the legal standard for agent IDs?"; asked what "semantic setting" means.
- Nikhil Swamy raised agent legal personhood as "a political question"; asked whether agents must first be "reliable enough that enterprises are willing to allow agents to act autonomously on their behalf."
- Philipp Jovanovic (UCL) endorsed the thesis but warned: many blockchain projects "develop technology for technology's sake... and have completely lost sight of the use case and the user" — build user-oriented from the start. "Bootstrapping a trust network is always one of the most difficult processes there is." Suggested a caching layer for data handling.
- Ke Wu asked about the TCP/IP adoption story and welcomed the fellowship's flexibility: "I believe in decentralization" while acknowledging it is "really hard."
- Angel Sanchez (Universidad Carlos III de Madrid, computational social science) connected OV to his social-norms research; shared behavioral experiments in Spain and China showing decisions are "motivated by economic considerations with a weight of only like 20%" — beliefs and social norms carry most of the weight, evidence for OV's non-purely-economic trust framing. Cited a Barcelona football club position paper on coordinating AI agents.
## Fellowship Program Feedback
- Bas Spitters: one-year postdocs are difficult to staff; PhD students or two-year postdocs are more typical. Accepted the flexible extension/bridge-funding model.
- Philipp Jovanovic: unrestricted gifts without IP claims are usually easiest for university funding and avoid large overheads. Asked how OV is financed and who the donors are — "the exact amount is less important than knowing which people and organizations are behind it."
- Nikhil Swamy (Microsoft employees ineligible as fellows) agreed to forward the explainer to academic colleagues and will consider joining the fellowship funding committee; called the initiative "awesome" and praised the broad-collaboration model.
- Everyone agreed to forward materials: Ning Luo, Ke Wu, Bas Spitters, Philipp Jovanovic, and Angel Sanchez all committed to sharing the primer with aligned researchers.
