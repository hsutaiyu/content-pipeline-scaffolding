# Digest — Oct 12–26, 2026

**TL;DR:** 14 calls. Tokenless incentive design — how to define utility, motivate participation and pay for infrastructure without a token — raised independently on at least six calls; proof compactness at Internet-scale throughput is the second hard technical objection.

Period: Oct 12 – Oct 26, 2026 · Calls covered: 14
Biggest recurring theme: the tokenless incentive question — how to define utility, motivate participation and pay for infrastructure without a token — raised independently on at least six calls (Garratt, Elkind, McAvoy, Nayak, Wadhwa, Rafael). Proof compactness at Internet-scale throughput is the second hard technical objection.
## Consensus & ordering layer: is it needed, is it possible
- Kartik Nayak (Duke) challenged the throughput target directly: even 400-gigabit links with 100 parties cannot support 100 billion transactions per second, "physics prevents it." He asked why existing consensus research does not already solve the content-blind ordering problem, and proposed encrypted payloads ordered first and decrypted afterward (with client, trusted-environment, or committee decryption) as a possible design. He also questioned transaction size and bandwidth/infrastructure costs.
- Dimitris Papadopoulos (HKUST) called the proposal "a whole game changer": "you have to rethink your whole stack, including your crypto stack" for multi-agent, nondeterministic interaction. He characterized it functionally as "IPFS plus ordering" and pressed on design assumptions: closed vs open network, fixed validator set, whether blockchain consensus is needed at all, and whether the trust graph comes from a separate base layer or from agents themselves.
- Ignacio "Nacho" Amores Sesar (Aarhus) questioned whether total ordering is necessary at all, suggesting pairwise/local ordering could simplify load. He works on duct-based consensus with asymmetric trust, motivated by proof-of-stake concentration ("people with a lot of money decide everything") and proof-of-work's environmental costs.
- Jörg Liesen (TU Berlin) asked whether the ordering layer is essentially "a universal blockchain," just verifiable timestamped transactions, and questioned real-world adoption: blockchain has struggled with the finance industry, plus digital-euro politics, centralization and surveillance concerns. On the positive side, he found the consensus-security-filter direction (generalizing his Stellar/FBA matrix analysis into a security filter for candidate ordering protocols) relevant and accepted it.
- Alejandro Aguirre (Aarhus) asked how the design prevents forged timestamps and forgeable reviews, questioned the synchrony assumptions behind timestamp consensus ("everything is asynchronous"), and asked whether the protocol itself has well-defined semantics even while staying agnostic to participants' semantics. He liked that OV cares about formal methods.
- Dahlia Malkhi (UCSB) confirmed strong alignment around erasure coding in storage ("Sounds like there's a good alignment around error correcting, coded erasure codes in storage") and interest in fundamental flaws in existing consensus protocols ("I'm definitely interested in that").
## Tokenless incentives, mechanism design & game theory
- Rod Garratt (UCSB) connected the problem to mechanism design, truth-telling and incentive compatibility, framing eliciting beneficial behavior without monetary payoffs as "a well-formulated mechanism design problem." His concrete suggestion: partition the broad problem into roughly 50 pieces rather than having 50 people each solve everything. He connected it to his IMF work with Tobias Adrian, "Trust Bridges and Money Flows" (why trust bridges are needed, but not how trust links get established), and to his Project Agora consulting.
- Edith Elkind (Northwestern) asked the sharpest set of modeling questions: who the actual players are (agents, controllers, or autonomous short-lived agents "that needn't have permanent identities"), whether maintainers and service customers are two different entity classes, and pointed out that even a token-free service means "the users of the system would have to pay into it somehow to make it feasible." She also asked exactly what blockchain components get stripped vs retained, and characterized the distributed guarantees as probabilistic. She called the vision compelling.
- Alex McAvoy (UNC Chapel Hill) noted "there's a relationship to mechanism design, which is... notoriously difficult to implement in practice," and pointed to deep-learning-era work on approximate incentives based on social welfare as the relevant recent thread. His own angle: emergent population-wide behavior from simple local choices, without centralized information or institutions, with spillover from evolutionary game theory into multi-agent RL.
- Sarisht Wadhwa (U Michigan) probed what "tokenless" actually means: "what if it is like Reddit where the value of token is null but they're still token?" He suggested social trust as a valued social construct a token could be linked to, and observed that application-layer incentives still exist even when execution is decoupled from ordering/data availability. His research: censorship and spam resistance solved together ("opposite sides of each other"), plus his advisor's network pricing work; he and his advisor are already drafting a proposal.
- Rafael Ziolkowski (U Zurich) observed that governance is still an organizational question "even without a token, or exactly because of it": who participates, identity, coordination. His own governance work is qualitative stakeholder/institutional design (Tezos technical advisory, the Carus Swiss auto-data consortium), so he asked whether the team has already spoken with game theorists and committed to find a quantitative/game-theory partner and draft a half-page idea paper.
- Kartik Nayak also questioned the economics: who participates, who runs the infrastructure, and what it costs to operate in a tokenless model.
## Post-quantum security & proof compactness
- Alexander Russell (UConn) flagged the proof-size gap: current requirements look "at least one, maybe even two" orders of magnitude away from the roughly 5 KB compact-proof target. He found the overall vision "a great dream" but said "you're setting your sights very high here." He also asked whether the trust graph itself provides a PKI or presupposes a public-key directory, and noted you "can imagine arbitrarily sophisticated Turing Complete contracts that mine out your trust graph."
- Ueli Maurer (ETH Zurich, emeritus, Concordium co-founder) presented his event algebra ("Event Algebras and Applications to Cryptography," IACR ePrint): a well-defined lattice where ordering statements between events are algebraic inequalities, letting protocol-correctness and crypto-security proofs be made "much simpler and at the same time much more general" without Turing machines or reductions. He pointed to his "New Approaches to Digital Evidence" paper (signatures, certificates, timestamps) as relevant to the compact, cryptographically provable event-representation question. He will review materials but is waiting to see the initiative's backing and donors before committing.
- Dimitris Papadopoulos called post-quantum security plus compactness reconciliation an open problem: "That's an open problem... how we're going to do that post quantum and compactness, reconciling them."
## Agent identity, trust graphs & trusted hardware
- Christina Garman (Purdue) pitched TEE-based, low-effort consensus/bootstrapping: nodes draw random values inside trusted execution environments (proof-of-elapsed-time style), smallest value wins the proposer slot, with attestation and deviation checking to catch compromised nodes. Her caveat: original SGX gave the needed guarantees, "modern systems do not do that," so hardened TEE infrastructure would itself be a research deliverable. She and Daniel Genkin (Georgia Tech) agreed to develop a joint pre-proposal (joint submission confirmed acceptable), and Christina agreed with the fully adversarial threat-model framing.
- Rod Garratt was interested in the cryptographically verifiable Yelp-review/trust-graph concept, but raised oracle-style manipulation and the difficulty of agreeing on information without manipulation, referencing an impossibility theorem.
- Dimitris Papadopoulos raised AI avatar/impersonation risks for agent identity and asked whether trusted hardware or hybrid crypto/TEE co-design should be considered alongside purely cryptographic approaches.
- Alejandro Aguirre asked how a third party establishes that an entity is trusted, and whether the parties need a shared agreement on how to communicate trust.
## Regulation, funding mechanics & program design feedback
- Rod Garratt (US stablecoin regulation, Congressional Budget Office adviser) on the banking analog: banks should be able to use public blockchains as an alternative payment rail, acting as collecting agents like they do for checks, but "the GENIUS Act is necessary and it helps, but it doesn't solve" the legal aspects of processing stablecoins. The gap is mapping banking law onto stablecoins, e.g. what the digital analog of signing a check even means. This is his live research area and shapes what an ordering layer would need to satisfy legally.
- Dahlia Malkhi pushed back on program structure: "the more structure and the more details we inject into this interaction, the more likely the university is going to step in and grab overhead of 50%." She prefers unrestricted gifts with no grant proposal or contract, built on trust. She also warned against "lip service" events: "if there's a way to form... actual collaborations... that is meaningful, that is important." Lab needs: a postdoc at about $70k/year, PhD students about $45k/year; she offered to host an in-person event at UCSB in May.
- Daniel Genkin asked for a funding ballpark up front ("otherwise it's too much of a degree of freedom about what we can propose") and confirmed a two-person joint submission is fine without broader university participation.
- Ignacio Sesar raised how funds flow through Danish university regulations and whether money goes to the university or the researcher; Andreas committed that OV negotiates so the requested budget reaches researchers in full, which Nacho accepted ("I'm definitely interested").
- Kartik Nayak asked about the funding committee's composition, whether IP/patent requirements would be problematic (he judged patenting unlikely in this area), and whether a website exists yet.
- Ayesha Kiani (Monarq AM / NYU) asked who can participate (grad/PhD students qualify), and noted her close collaborators are at NYU Stern while her CS collaborators mostly don't work on distributed networks; she'll gather current and former students and identify NYU colleagues, with materials sent to her Monarq address.
## Open questions for R&D
1. What is the actual throughput/transaction-size budget, and is 100B+ tx/sec physically attainable given bandwidth limits? (Kartik)
1. Can payloads be ordered encrypted and decrypted post-ordering, and who decrypts? (Kartik)
1. Is total ordering required, or is pairwise/local ordering sufficient for the target use cases? (Nacho)
1. What exactly do participants pay, and how do maintainers get compensated, in a tokenless model? (Elkind, Kartik)
1. Can a non-monetary social construct (social trust, reputation) substitute for token-based incentives? (Wadhwa, Garratt)
1. How do we prevent forged timestamps and forgeable reviews/trust attestations, especially under full asynchrony? (Alejandro)
1. Can compact post-quantum proofs hit the about 5 KB target, given the current 1-2 order-of-magnitude gap? (Russell, Dimitris)
1. Does the trust graph provide the PKI or presuppose a public-key directory? (Russell, Dimitris)
1. Should trusted hardware / hybrid TEE-crypto co-design be part of the stack, and what would hardened TEEs need to guarantee? (Christina, Dimitris)
1. Should the research program be partitioned into roughly 50 well-defined sub-problems for the fellow cohort? (Garratt)
1. What funding structure (unrestricted gift vs structured grant) works per institution, given overhead differences like UCSB's roughly 50% and Danish regulations? (Dahlia, Nacho)
