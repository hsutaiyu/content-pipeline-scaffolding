# Recurring ideas

<!-- Snapshot of the “Low level content” sheet (research-call lines, as of Oct 12, 2026). Ported 2026-10-21. -->

Thirteen ideas carry almost every call. Andreas makes the problem concrete with agents, telcos and open questions; Jack draws the architecture in a few phrases that travel well in one sentence.

| Idea | Andreas | Jack |
|---|---|---|
| The thesis | “safe and neutral coordination of economic value exchanges between heterogeneous economic actors” (every call) | “neutral and safe economic coordination tech” (Steven Galbraith, notes) |
| Why there are a thousand chains | “if humans were to be amenable to one semantic set, we would have one world language” (Fernando Pedone, notes) | “we are radically decoupling computation from coordination” (Abba Wada, notes) |
| What the base layer does | “This is just event ordering... everything else becomes reasoning about that event.” (Leonid Reyzin, notes) | “it should not validate anything, it should not execute anything... it’s an event log” (Charalampos Papamanthou, notes) |
| What it asks of other systems | “everyone can keep on running their state machines and all they need to do is export a publicly verifiable proof of something that happened” (Jérémie Decouchant, notes) | “nobody has to inherit each other’s execution environments or use a token to coordinate with each other” (Dazza Greenwood, notes) |
| Value lives on intranets | Not his phrase | No real Internet of Value yet, only “intranets of value” passing signed messages over the Internet of information (Dan Bernstein, Steven Galbraith, notes) |
| Reference, not handshake | “peer to... bulletin board to peer” rather than peer to trust bridge to peer (Vero Estrada-Galiñanes, notes) | “a peer to publicly verifiable bulletin board to peer reference protocol” (Scott Shenker, Jérémie Decouchant, notes) |
| Additive | “It does not intermediate trust. It allows to negotiate trust between counterparties.” (Illini Blockchain, notes) | “We’re an additive protocol. We’re not trying to replace anyone. We’re trying to add.” (Joshua Gancher, notes) |
| Why tokenless | An internet service can’t comply with a few hundred financial regulatory frameworks at once (most calls) | “the access points cannot be like token gated” (Alysson Bessani, transcript) |
| Internet anchors | Root DNS servers and ICANN, TCP/IP, TLS (most calls) | “there’s no validity of any of the events. It’s just all sort of attestations of proofs... for people to review” (James Grimmelmann, notes) |
| Agents as employees | Legal identity, power of attorney, proof of correct execution; “an agent is basically an employee” (Leonard Rosenthol, notes) | “agents of agents of agents underneath for different scope of authorization” (Lynn LoPucki, transcript) |
| Trust graphs | “a cryptographically verifiable version of Yelp reviews” (Atul Prakash and most later calls) | Not his phrase |
| Candor about the unknowns | “We don’t even know all the questions, right? Let alone be able to build it.” (Pratyush Mishra, notes) | “that’s the million dollar question, if you will. Literally.” (Marten van Dijk, notes) |
| Where it came from | About 20 researchers concluded the problem was too big for one team, so they opened it up (Lorenzo Grassi, Carmit Hazay, notes) | “we modernized bitcoin, made it pqs and gave it high tps... this is completely different. This is not even a block. This is a new Internet protocol now.” (Alysson Bessani, transcript) |
