# Questions researchers ask

<!-- Snapshot of the “Low level content” sheet (research-call lines, as of Oct 12, 2026). Ported 2026-10-21. -->

Sixteen questions about the thesis and nine about the program came up across the calls, and each one is FAQ, blog or explainer material. Where no answer landed on the call, the third column says so.

| Topic | Question | Asked by | Answer that worked |
|---|---|---|---|
| About the thesis | Why not use an existing blockchain as the data store? | Kartik Nayak, Duke · Aug 3; Fernando Pedone (“isn’t Ethereum here to disprove you?”) · Sep 25 | Chains make everyone compete for the same block space under one set of semantics, and they don’t scale to this volume. Credit first: Ethereum showed a common environment can succeed, and the thousand chains since show not every system will share one. |
| About the thesis | Why not sharded chains, or certificate chains of proofs with no global order? | Jay Lorch, Microsoft Research · Sep 15 | Certificate chains suit human-paced processes. Agent-built supply chains, like a Singapore to New York network deal assembled in seconds, may need a global order. Andreas left it open. |
| About the thesis | Which kind of trust do you mean? | Leonid Reyzin, Boston University · Aug 17 | “This is just event ordering... everything else becomes reasoning about that event.” Then: necessary, not sufficient. |
| About the thesis | Why a bulletin board instead of talking directly? | Lynn LoPucki, UF Law · Jul 27 | Did not land. The answer that should lead: a central coordinator per use case works well, but corporation to corporation, the systems of record drift apart. |
| About the thesis | Can’t parties collude to fake a history, or build a reputation and then defect? | Atul Prakash, Michigan · Sep 21; Ghassan Karame (“Mafia attack”) · Sep 28 | Identities anchored to a company’s own domain put its brand at stake, and a graph weighted across many counterparties is hard to game. The defection case stays open. |
| About the thesis | Won’t two agents read the same history differently? | Atul Prakash · Sep 21 | No answer recorded. Suggested: yes, by design. The base layer orders and each party judges. Check with Andreas. |
| About the thesis | Who runs the nodes, and who pays them? | Fernando Pedone · Sep 25; Manuel Bravo, Informal Systems · Aug 27; Adam Rusch, UIUC · Sep 4 | “There is no business model.” The root server operators run the Internet’s root because their own business sits on top of it. Adam’s version: merchants build the roads they need. |
| About the thesis | Without a token, what keeps nodes honest and stops fake identities? | Alysson Bessani · Aug 25; Pratyush Mishra · Sep 25; Rüdiger Kapitza, FAU · Aug 12; Ke Wu · Oct 6 | Open, and the first cohort’s main question. Ideas floated: KYC-gated operators, ejecting nodes that misbehave, time delays or work to join, paid availability tiers “just like an API endpoint”. |
| About the thesis | If an agent is steered into fraud, how do you rewind it? | Ari Trachtenberg, Boston University · Aug 10 | No answer recorded. Suggested: the base layer only orders; reversal and disputes stay with the systems that settle. Check with Andreas. |
| About the thesis | Doesn’t fast value transfer make scams faster too? | Leonid Reyzin · Aug 17 | “A neutral root of trust is just a necessary requirement, it’s not a sufficient requirement.” Identity, scope and liability sit above it. |
| About the thesis | Won’t anchored trails expose private business? | Atul Prakash · Sep 21; Roozbeh, KU Leuven · Sep 8; Scott Shenker · Aug 11 | Proofs of correct execution keep inputs private, no personal data needs anchoring, and pseudonymity is enough. |
| About the thesis | What about illegal content, takedowns and GDPR? | Shabsi Walfish, Google · Sep 17; Rolf Weber, University of Zurich · Sep 3 | Open. Which nodes pin which data is the question, and legal scholars are part of the program. |
| About the thesis | Is this like x402? | Arnav Chandak, Illini Blockchain · Sep 14 | Layer placement, settled on Sep 9: x402 handles payments at the semantic layer; the base layer is a reference protocol for auditability and coordination. Not a competitor. |
| About the thesis | How does a record stop someone lying about the physical world? | Jay Lorch (steel relabeled through a third country) · Sep 15 | It doesn’t, fully. It sets a chain of liability. Pair with the steel scene in Bucket 1. |
| About the thesis | Does the order have to match real time? | Jay Lorch · Sep 15 | Provable order within one batch’s uncertainty; local and eventual ordering are on the table. |
| About the thesis | Don’t OAuth and trust brokers already handle this without consensus? | Shabsi Walfish · Sep 17 | Andreas: they are domain-specific and bespoke. Shabsi stayed unconvinced of the killer use case, since banks are happy owning their ledgers. Needs a sharper answer. |
| About the program | Is my work a fit? | Lorenzo Grassi · Sep 29; Josh Benaloh · Sep 16; Aaron Stump · Aug 18; Dominique Unruh · Sep 16 | Name the open problem in their field for them. Dominique found one live on the call: time-stamped classical proofs to ease the move to post-quantum. |
| About the program | Will the money materialize? | Daniel Genkin (“do you know that it’s going to get funded?”) · Sep 17; Stefano Tessaro, University of Washington · Aug 27 | A Swiss non-profit, gifts pledged, donors checked and disclosed to Swiss authorities. Say it plainly. |
| About the program | How are proposals judged? | Ari Trachtenberg · Aug 10; Vero Estrada-Galiñanes · Aug 13 | Published criteria, a committee drawn from enterprise research labs, and review together before submission. |
| About the program | Sprints don’t fit PhD timelines. | Nigel Smart · Aug 10; Vincent Rijmen · Aug 25; Ling Ren · Aug 31; David Starobinski · Sep 3 | One-year fellowships, extendable, with options for postdocs and bridge funding for PhD students. |
| About the program | Gift or grant? What about overhead? | David Starobinski · Sep 3; Anirudh Sivaraman · Sep 29; Christian · Aug 27 | Unrestricted gifts with no IP claims, worked out with each university. |
| About the program | Is this an RFP that’s already decided? | Ari Trachtenberg (“the faculty are fairly jaded”) · Aug 10 | Open licensing, published criteria and collaborative review answer it. |
| About the program | How much unpaid work is the pre-proposal? | Rolf Weber · Sep 3; Fernando Pedone · Sep 25; Clara Schneidewind · Sep 14 | One to two pages. An AI agent can draft one in about five minutes, per a researcher reported in the Sep 14 sales sync. |
