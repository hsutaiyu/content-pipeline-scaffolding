# What’s landing

<!-- Snapshot of the “Low level content” sheet (research-call lines, as of Oct 12, 2026). Ported 2026-10-21. -->

The thesis itself rarely drew pushback. Interest turned on three things: whether it touched the researcher’s own open problem, whether the token question was answered early, and how concrete the ask was.

| Result | Pattern | What we heard | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Landed | “Ordering only” reads as infrastructure, not crypto | Scott Shenker (ICSI Berkeley): “I think I got too hung up on the word layer. This is sort of what I would just call a universal service.” Mahesh Balakrishnan (Meta): “It’s always puzzled me why one of the practical outcomes of blockchain was not what I’d call a trusted log.” Mario Larangeira and Zhaozhong Ni restated it unprompted as a thinner layer one, a log with no state. | Lead with what the base layer does and does not do. With networking and systems people, say “universal service” rather than “layer”. |
| Landed | Their own open problem is the hook | Pratyush Mishra’s work shrinking post-quantum proofs met the sub-5 KB target head on. Ke Wu had read the call and saw a direct match with research on rewarding base-layer participants. Mohammad Sadoghi called agentic transactions “probably for my next five years”. Dominique Unruh only engaged once the call reached an angle of Dominique’s own: time-stamped validity of classical proofs. | Every piece names one field’s open problem: consensus, data availability, post-quantum proofs, mechanism design, fairness, law. |
| Landed | Spell out the field and the question lands | Omer Reingold (Stanford) first said: “What I still don’t see... is exactly what is the fairness question that arise here.” After Andreas walked fairness through leader election, inclusion, sampling and archiving: “that makes a lot of sense.” Ling Ren asked for “here’s the challenge, here is the open problem, here is where we need help.” | Open problems as numbered items, each tied to a field. |
| Landed | No token, no IP, a non-profit: the trust question goes away | Ling Ren avoids projects that get “tunneled into the coin price”, and the tokenless model resonated. Yale’s last token-funded research center was cut after one year when the token fell. Stefano Tessaro said universities are wary of blockchain money. Philipp Jovanovic and Bas Spitters welcomed MIT and CC0 licensing with no IP retained. | Say “no token” and “everything open” early in anything aimed at academics. |
| Landed | Agent liability lands with people building agents | Mahesh Balakrishnan: “The liability for agents is the first and most important problem right now.” Leonard Rosenthol (Adobe): “at the moment we don’t have a model for agentic identity”. Nikhil Swamy, Ning Luo and Atul Prakash each tied it to their own agent work. | The employee anchor: identity, scope, and proof of what the agent did. |
| Landed | Candor earns trust | Ángel Sánchez: “you got me really hooked on the idea and on the way you want to handle the projects or the proposals.” Ke Wu called impossibility results a big part of theory, so “a negative answer... bounds the solution space” landed. Gil Segev warned against over-positioning against goals not known to be achievable. | Research tense. Invite negative results. Say “we don’t know” where it is true. |
| Landed | The collaborative review is a differentiator | Claudio Tessone preferred it to blind submission. Ning Luo called the program “an academic version of Y Combinator”. Ari Trachtenberg: faculty “need to understand how proposals are being judged... before they’re willing to put in their newer ideas.” | Program content leads with how review works. |
| Landed | Others are converging on the same idea | Pedro Moreno-Sanchez’s team at Visa Research had sketched an “Internet of Value” of its own. Ángel Sánchez had received a position paper on coordinating AI agents the week before. Scott Shenker linked the idea to shared-log research. | Why now, stated as convergence, per the canonical plan. |
| Did not land | Agents and identity before the business problem | Lynn LoPucki (UF Law): “Why does somebody need this bulletin board instead of direct communication?” and later “You’ve lost me entirely.” Jack, in the debrief: “spell out the business processes, the business logic at the enterprise level before we even talk about agents.” | Open on a finance, telco or supply-chain scene. Agents come second. |
| Did not land | “Why not just use a blockchain?” | Kartik Nayak (Duke) asked why existing chains can’t be the data store. Fernando Pedone: “isn’t Ethereum here to disprove you?” Jay Lorch (Microsoft Research) proposed sharded chains or certificate chains instead. | A credit-first answer ready wherever a piece invites the question (see Questions researchers ask). |
| Did not land | Speed words collide with academic time | Nigel Smart: “a short sprint is useless for a PhD student... For a PhD student a year is a short sprint.” Vincent Rijmen: no professor puts a student on a problem someone else may solve next month. Ling Ren has problems open for “six and seven years”. | Say cohorts and one-year fellowships, not sprints. |
| Did not land | “Blockchain” carries baggage | Gerhard Schwabe: “the only use case that I really see is fraud and illegal transactions”. Ling Ren: “a winter of blockchain”. Andreas to the Yale group: “stay away from the word blockchain.” | Internet words only. |
| Did not land | Abstract “trust” invites a fight | Leonid Reyzin asked which trust: identity, timestamping, money movement or archives. Leonid also warned that fast value transfer alone “may get you on a faster train to hell.” | Name the narrow guarantee, and say it is necessary, not sufficient. |
| Did not land | A vague ask | Daniel Genkin: “what do you guys... want from me… you want me to submit a grant?” Mahesh Balakrishnan: “How do you want me to help?” | End on one concrete next step. |
| The internal read matches. Jack on August 14: “The thesis actually does resonate with a lot of people.” On October 12, about 24 researchers had said they will submit a pre-proposal, and the first one was in. |  |  |  |
